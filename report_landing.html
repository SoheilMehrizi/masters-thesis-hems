Literature review

Review expanded from the latest related-work manuscript and two HVAC diagnosis papers. Select a row to jump to its reference. Imported manuscript references still require independent bibliographic checking.

CategoryKey paperFocus

Agentic HVAC diagnosis — Li et al. (2026)

Combines an AI agent, a building/control ontology, and generalized rules to distinguish faults involving sensors, actuators, load, upstream plant, and setpoints. Its real-system evaluation supports interpretable diagnosis within the tested HVAC loops.

Relevance: diagnostic agents should ground their reports in structured system knowledge and explicit rules. The reported HVAC results do not establish general residential HEMS recovery performance.

Full reference and DOI

Cross-level fault isolation — Huang et al. (2024)

EECL learns Bayesian-network structure from fault/symptom data to isolate faults that propagate between coupled HVAC subsystems. The paper compares isolation performance and model complexity across three test cases.

Relevance: evaluate competing root-cause hypotheses rather than treating every symptom as a local fault. Transfer to preference, forecast, and coordination failures requires new validation and explicit causal assumptions.

Full reference and DOI

Novelty and overlap

We build on existing work, but propose a different focus and mechanism.

Already covered

  • LLM for preference translation
  • Reward design with LLMs
  • Warm-starting RL
  • Constraint-grounded schedule diagnosis and repair (DARC)
  • Digital-twin diagnosis and model updates (STAR)
  • Agentic and Bayesian-network HVAC diagnosis

Our proposed contribution

Test whether diagnosis can select the right recovery action across preference, forecast, model, device-state, and coordination failures.

The earlier train-versus-refine loop remains Method v1; the broader agentic digital-twin direction is proposed as Method v2.

To be verified

  • Degree of improvement over related work
  • Uniqueness of the diagnostic mechanism
  • Full comparison with closest methods

Novelty remains provisional: an LLM interface, digital twin, multi-agent control, or diagnosis loop alone is already represented in related work. The proposed contribution is evidence-grounded selection among different recovery actions under ambiguous symptoms and model uncertainty.

Proposed methodology

Diagnostic-driven loop for efficient adaptation.

Adaptive
training loop

Proposed: diagnosis-guided intervention selection

Monitor observations and prediction residuals; compare a finite set of fault hypotheses using historical traces, deterministic constraint checks, and digital-twin simulations. The LLM coordinates tools and explains evidence; numerical control and constraint enforcement remain with the optimizer and checker.

HypothesisCandidate response
Preference driftClarify or recalibrate preferences
Forecast biasUpdate the forecast model
Twin parameter mismatchEstimate parameters and recalibrate the twin
Device-state inconsistencyResynchronize state estimates
Schedule or coordination failureReplan or repair agent/tool coordination

When evidence is inconclusive, collect further observations or use conservative fallback. Twin counterfactuals are model-based evidence; physical causality requires explicit assumptions and validation. This architecture has not yet been implemented.

Reproducible evaluation protocol

  1. Freeze software version, dataset identifier, schema, checksums, and random seeds.
  2. Use chronological training, validation, and held-out test periods; prevent future observations leaking into forecasts.
  3. Run a fault-free control case, then inject preference changes, forecast bias, twin mismatch, stale states, and coordination failures.
  4. Record fault type, severity, onset, duration, and affected subsystem; test single and concurrent faults.
  5. Keep optimizer, observations, and budgets comparable across diagnosis and replanning baselines.
