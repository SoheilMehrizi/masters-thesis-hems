H I G H L I G H T S
• A large language model-enhanced home energy management framework is proposed, integrating Large Language Models with Deep Reinforcement Learning.
• Knowledge-guided chain-of-thought method is introduced to achieve precise, real-time user preference perception via natural language interaction.
• LLM-generated reference actions are leveraged to guide DRL agent, accelerating convergence and improving performance under limited data conditions.
• A hierarchical data-sharing mechanism is developed to protect user privacy, utilizing LLMs for knowledge extraction and efficient data selection.

A B S T R A C T
The residential sector has received great attention worldwide due to its significant contribution to energy con­
sumption and carbon emissions. Although Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) based Home Energy Management 
(HEM) has provided promising solutions for user comfort satisfaction and energy savings, existing approaches 
still face three challenges: (1) accurately perceiving dynamic user preferences, (2) efficient learning with limited 
data samples, and (3) protecting user privacy during data sharing. To address these issues, a Large Language 
Model (LLM)-enhanced HEM with dynamic user preference elicitation and hierarchical data-sharing is proposed, 
introducing three key innovations. First, the Knowledge-Guided Chain-of-Thought method is proposed to per­
ceive dynamic user preferences from unconstrained natural language interactions, by leveraging users’ historical 
knowledge as explicit guidance. Second, the LLM-generated reference actions based on shared knowledge and 
preference descriptions are proposed to accelerate the learning process under conditions of cold-start situations. 
Third, a hierarchical data-sharing mechanism integrated with LLM-based knowledge extraction is introduced to 
enable effective knowledge inference from natural language while preserving user privacy. Simulation results in 
the household scenario with dynamic user preferences demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves sig­
nificant improvements in both user preference elicitation and learning efficiency, ultimately leading to a 28.8% 
reduction in cost and a 9.3% improvement in comfort compared to fixed preference DRL methods.
1 . Introduction
The continuous growth in global energy consumption needs has in­
tensified environmental pollution and climate change, leading to a shift 
toward greener energy systems. The residential energy sector is a crit­
ical area for low-carbon transitions [ 1] due to its high proportion of 
total energy consumption, and its energy demand is still rising. In 2019, 
residential electricity consumption accounted for 26.6% of total elec­
tricity consumption [ 2]. The Home Energy Management (HEM) plays 
a crucial role in enhancing home energy efficiency by optimizing en­
ergy usage, improving reliability, and integrating renewable energy 
sources [3,4].

Current approaches to HEM primarily fall into two paradigms: 
model-based and data-driven. Model-based energy management meth­
ods reduce total energy costs by modelling and controlling the energy 
usage of a house while meeting the comfort requirements of occu­
pants [5–7]. A typical model-based method is Model Predictive Control 
(MPC) [8], which first predicts model parameters and then optimizes 
control actions using a mathematical solver. To integrate the PV-battery, 
the scenario-based stochastic MPC is applied  [ 9]. Also, adaptive ro­
bust MPC is utilized by introducing an additional uncertainty model to 
represent load disturbances  [10]. Moreover, reference [11] introduced 
MPC through Monte Carlo uncertainty quantification and vehicle-to-
home communication protocols. Although model-based methods offer 
a promising solution for HEM, these methods rely on precise modelling 
and struggle to adapt to dynamic preferences, which prevents achieving 
optimal performance of HEM in the high uncertainty environment.
Data-driven approaches have transformed HEM by eliminating mod­
elling dependencies while enhancing adaptability [ 12]. Reinforcement 
Learning (RL) is the typical method that can minimize home energy 
cost in a dynamic environment [ 13,14]. An important research direc­
tion of RL-based HEM is to consider the application in a more complex 
environment, such as extending to multi-energy coordination  [ 15,16], 
considering real-time demand response for optimal scheduling of home 
appliances  [ 17,18], and dealing with the uncertainty of electricity 
prices [19]. Also, the learning efficiency and performance are improved 
through reward shaping  [ 20] and combining a knowledge-driven 
model [21]. Recently, the new attempt is to incorporate the personal­
ized preferences in the HEM process, such as customers’ and operators’ 
perspectives  [ 22], and residents’ feedback and activity [ 23]. Despite 
the significant improvements in the approaches, existing data-driven 
methods still face two major challenges. First, difficulty in accurately 
perceiving users’ dynamic numerical preferences, or preference elicita­
tion, through a human-friendly, unconstrained natural language dialog. 
Second, the low learning efficiency and performance occur under condi­
tions of limited data samples, especially when the environment and user 
preferences change.
Recent advancements in Large Language Model (LLM) have demon­
strated strong capabilities in natural language understanding, reasoning, 
and judgment [24,25]. On one hand, LLMs have emerged as a promis­
ing approach to augment RL, referred to as LLM-enhanced RL, improving 
multitask learning, sample efficiency, and high-level task planning [26]. 
The Action-Guider paradigm of LLM-enhanced RL demonstrates how 
LLMs improve learning efficiency through two key mechanisms. First, 
for large action spaces, such as text-based games, LLMs directly gen­
erate high-value action candidates [ 27,28]. Second, LLMs improve the 
quality of policy-generated actions by utilizing inherent expert knowl­
edge [29,30]. On the other hand, for the HEM, the natural language 
understanding and reasoning capabilities of LLMs enable precise user 
preference elicitation through unconstrained natural language. This, in 
turn, assists RL agents in formulating a more accurate reward func­
tion and addresses the traditional difficulty of modelling personalized 
preferences in RL-based HEM.
Building on these insights, an innovative LLM-enhanced HEM frame­
work with dynamic user preference elicitation and a hierarchical 
data-sharing mechanism is proposed, which introduces four major con­
tributions:
1. Innovative LLM-Enhanced HEM Framework: A novel control 
framework that synergistically integrates LLM, RL, and a data-
sharing mechanism is proposed to address the core challenges of 
dynamic user preference elicitation, efficient learning, and privacy 
protection in RL-based HEM.
2. Dynamic User Preference Elicitation: To accurately perceive dy­
namic user preferences through unconstrained natural language 
interaction, the Knowledge-Guided Chain-of-Thought (KG-CoT) 
reasoning is proposed by leveraging users’ historical knowledge 
as explicit guidance and incorporating a reflection mechanism, 
enabling the accurate capture of hourly comfort and objective 
numerical changes in users.
3. LLM-enhanced Action Guidance: To mitigate cold-start explo­
ration risks in home energy systems, the LLM-enhanced action 
guidance mechanism which leverages relevant knowledge from 
similar users is proposed. By transferring such cross-user knowl­
edge, the mechanism accelerates RL learning under limited data 
samples and improves energy management performance in envi­
ronments with dynamic user preferences.
4. Hierarchical Data-Sharing Mechanism: A hierarchical data-sharing 
mechanism integrated with LLM-based knowledge extraction is 
proposed to enable effective knowledge inference from uncon­
strained natural language while preserving user privacy, which 
facilitates efficient and targeted data selection for downstream 
learning and decision-making.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. An overview of 
the HEM model is introduced in Section 2. Then, a detailed method­
ology of our proposed framework is presented in Section 3. Section 4 
demonstrates the framework’s control performance through comprehen­
sive simulation results. Section 5 concludes with key findings and future 
research directions.
2 . Home energy management modelling
HEM needs to monitor and control energy generation, storage, and 
consumption within a household. HEM usually optimizes for a goal 
such as cost reduction, self-sufficiency maximization, or emissions mini­
mization. With the increasing adoption of electric mobility and heating, 
residential PV, and dynamic tariffs, HEM is gaining popularity as the sav­
ings potential increases. The models normally include renewable energy 
generation, battery energy storage, and heat transfer.
Fig. 1 illustrates the modular architecture of the HEM system. The 
core of the system is the HEM Controller, which is responsible for mak­
ing intelligent energy management decisions. It perceives the current 
environmental state by collecting information from both indoor (e.g., 
indoor temperature, power usage) and the external system (e.g., outdoor 
temperature, real-time electricity prices). Based on this information, the 
controller sends control signals to the physical components within the 
home environment, including the renewable energy generation, energy 
storage, and controllable loads. The ultimate goal of this control process 
is to optimize a set of predefined long-term objectives, namely energy 
cost, carbon emissions, and user comfort. The detailed mathematical 
models for the key components within the environment are presented 
in the following subsections.

3.1.2 . Overall framework
To address the core challenges of accurately perceiving dynamic user 
preferences, overcoming the slow convergence of traditional reinforce­
ment learning, and protecting user privacy, this paper proposes a com­
prehensive LLM-enhanced HEM framework. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the 
architecture is structured around three functionally distinct yet deeply 
interconnected modules: the Dynamic Preference Elicitation Module for 
understanding user preferences; the Energy Management Module for ef­
ficient energy management, and a Hierarchical Data Sharing Module for 
leveraging other users’ data while preserving privacy. These modules 
collaborate synergistically: the preference elicitation module defines 
the optimization objective, the data-sharing mechanism provides gen­
eralized strategic knowledge, and the Energy Management module 
synthesizes this information to execute and learn the optimal control 
policy. In this framework, we assume that all LLM modules that process 
user data (both raw and abstracted data) are deployed on local devices 
within the user’s home network, mitigating LLM-related privacy risks.

For the hourly control loop, at each time step, the RL Agent sub­
module generates the base action according to the state received from 
the environment. Concurrently, the agent determines if LLM inter­
vention is needed. If so, an LLM expert consults both generalized 
Shared Knowledge and user-specific Personal Knowledge to generate a 
high-quality reference action via the Action Guidance submodule. This 
reference is then intelligently blended with the base action to form the 
final control decision sent to the household Environment. The next state 
and cost information are further received, stored, and processed by the 
Reward Computing submodule, which calculates the reward based on 
the user’s current preferences as determined by the Dynamic Preference 
Elicitation Module. This complete experience tuple is then used to train 
the Actor and Critic networks, continuously refining the agent’s policy. 
The detailed algorithm is shown in Section 3.3.
For the daily data-sharing process, at the end of each operational cy­
cle (e.g., 24 o’clock), the system extracts knowledge from raw system 
information (such as control trajectories) and the current preference in­
formation. An LLM-based two-stage knowledge extraction is performed: 
first, it converts this specific, private data into anonymized textual de­
scriptions. Second, it further generalizes these descriptions into Shared 
Knowledge without private information. This knowledge extraction pro­
cess ensures that valuable control strategies can be shared among a 
community of users to improve collective performance without com­
promising any individual’s privacy. The shared knowledge becomes a 
key input for the Data Selection process, which selects the suitable 
knowledge for the Action Guidance submodule, allowing it to make 
more informed decisions. The detailed process is further elaborated in 
Section 3.4.
For the seasonal elicitation process, the system actively interacts 
with the user through unconstrained natural language to elicit user 
preferences. When the user provides feedback, the Knowledge-Guided 
Chain-of-Thought (KG-CoT) reasoning process is applied to infer the 
preference step by step in the preference inference submodule. After 
achieving the updated preference, the system will determine whether 
to conduct the second round of interaction. If the second round of user 
feedback is obtained, the preference evaluation submodule will evaluate 
and modify the updated preference to improve the accuracy. The final 
outcome is a precise update to the user’s Preference Information, which 
is immediately applied to the Reward Computing in the energy man­
agement module and informs the knowledge extraction process in the 
data-sharing module. The detailed process is introduced in Section 3.2.
3.2 . LLM-based dynamic preference elicitation
To address the limitations of obtaining accurately user preferences, 
this paper proposes the following LLM-based dynamic preference elic­
itation framework. The two-round interaction is designed within the 
framework. The first round feedback is used to infer the user preference, 
and the second round feedback is used to evaluate and further improve 
the accuracy of the inference result. The LLM functions as the reasoning 
engine in the process. The detailed process is shown in Fig. 3.
The first round of interaction is triggered when any of the following 
conditions occur: seasonal transitions happen, the percentage of neigh­
bors exhibiting preference changes exceeds the threshold 𝛽, or manual 
modifications are made to appliance states. The preference inference 
process begins by capturing the user’s natural language feedback (NL1). 
Then KG-CoT reasoning process is proposed to transform the natural 
language feedback into numerical preferences through three sequen­
tial, specialized reasoning steps, each guided by knowledge graphs to 
enhance precision and stability: (1) KG-CoT1 establishes the temporal 
scope for affected actions. (2) KG-CoT2 identifies the variable name 
and the direction of modification intended by the natural language. 
(3) KG-CoT3 updates memory using historical control patterns. The 
KG-CoT3 is required to output preference values and thermal comfort 
values in JSON format, facilitating the comprehension and utilization of 
RL. A JSON example is: `weights': {`alpha': 1.2, `beta': 0.09,

3.3 . LLM-enhanced DDPG algorithm
To enhance the DRL agent’s learning efficiency and performance 
for HEM with dynamic user preferences, this paper proposes an LLM-
enhanced DDPG algorithm with the action guidance. The LLM acts as 
an expert advisor, providing high-quality reference actions to steer the 
DDPG agent’s exploration, especially during the early stages of learn­
ing or after a significant change in the environment or user preferences. 
This collaborative approach accelerates convergence and helps the agent 
avoid suboptimal policies. To adapt this hybrid discrete-continuous ac­
tion space to the DDPG algorithm, we employ a relaxation-thresholding 
mapping. The actor network outputs a continuous vector 𝑎 ∈ [−1 , 1]4, 
which is mapped to physical actions as follows: 𝐴𝐶𝑜𝑛 = I(𝑎[0] > 0),

The I is the indicator function. This relaxation-thresholding approach 
maintains a unified continuous action space for DDPG efficiency while

3.4 . Hierarchical data sharing with LLM-based knowledge extraction
To protect user privacy while ensuring sufficient historical data sup­
ports LLMs and RL in making optimal action selections, the mechanism 
faces the challenge of balancing privacy and information sharing. To 
solve this problem, the paper proposes the hierarchical data sharing with 
LLM-based knowledge extraction, as illustrated in Fig. 4. Two key sub­
modules are applied to support hierarchical data sharing: Knowledge 
Extraction and Data Selection.
LLM-based knowledge extraction is the core process that enables 
privacy-preserving data sharing, which involves a two-stage abstraction 
performed by an LLM, namely, from raw information to natural language 
description, and then from natural language description to privacy-
preserving knowledge. To improve the effectiveness of knowledge ex­
traction, the data is divided into two parallel parts, user preference

System Information (e.g., control trajectories, energy consumption val­

the System Description and Preference Description, which are textual 
summaries of the raw data generated by the LLM. The last level stores 
generalized System Knowledge and Preference Knowledge, which is 
anonymized, stripped of all specific numerical values and personally 
identifiable patterns, and represents generalized control strategies and 
preference-driven insights. The strict separation ensures that only non-
sensitive, high-level knowledge is shared, forming the foundation of the 
privacy-preserving architecture.
The detailed LLM-based knowledge extraction process is shown as 
follows. For preference-related data, the process begins when the LLM 
receives the user’s raw Preference Information (Flow ➀). It first trans­
forms this numerical data into a qualitative, natural language Preference 
Description. This description captures the essence of the user’s priorities 
(e.g., “prioritizes comfort heavily in the evening”) without revealing 
the exact weightings. In the second stage (Flow ➁), the LLM further 
generalizes this description into actionable Preference Knowledge (e.g., 
“for users who prioritize evening comfort, increasing the heating set­
point is a valid strategy”). A parallel process occurs for system-related 
data. The LLM takes the raw System Information from the user’s op­
erational history (Flow ➂) and synthesizes it into a System Description, 
summarizing the agent’s behavior. Subsequently (Flow ➃), it distills this 
description into generalized System Knowledge, representing effective 
control heuristics (e.g., “pre-cooling during off-peak hours is an effective 
cost-saving measure”). Only the final, the doubly-abstracted Preference 
Knowledge and System Knowledge are saved to the Shared Memory 
(Flow ➄) at the end of day if LLM-based knowledge extraction is in­
voked on that day. To manage the memory size effectively, the Shared 
Memory is capped at 64 items. A three-tiered partition deletion strategy 
is employed when this limit is reached: the oldest section has the most 
data removed, the middle section has a moderate amount of data re­
moved, and the most recent section has the least data removed, thereby 
striking a balance between timeliness and diversity.
Data selection process (Flow ➅) ensures the effective usage of the 
shared memory. Since there is a large amount of knowledge in the shared 
memory from other users, agents should filter out the most relevant sug­
gestions and prioritize the most beneficial ones from the shared memory. 
As the preference data is most relevant and useful to the energy man­
agement strategy, only the user preference information is used in the 
data selection process. More specifically, the LLM selects the data from 
shared memory by comparing the user’s personal preference descrip­
tion and other users’ preference knowledge. The most similar preference 
knowledge from other users is selected by LLM, and the corresponding 
shared memory is used as the selected knowledge.

5 . Conclusion
This paper presents an LLM-enhanced HEM framework that inte­
grates dynamic user preference elicitation and the hierarchical data-
sharing mechanism. First, Knowledge-Guided Chain-of-Thought is pro­
posed to achieve precise and efficient hourly thermal comfort and 
objective preference elicitation. Second, the LLM-generated reference 
actions are introduced to accelerate RL under limited data conditions. 
Third, a hierarchical data-sharing mechanism with LLM-based knowl­
edge extraction is designed to protect user privacy while enabling 
efficient data selection. Experimental results demonstrate that the pro­
posed framework significantly outperforms the existing methods in both 
adaptability and control effectiveness. In particular, the costs are re­
duced by more than 28.8% and the comfort is improved by over 9.3% 
compared to fix preference RL method.
Future research will pursue several key directions to advance this 
work, including extending the current framework to multi-agent collabo­
rative scenarios, conducting in-depth analyses of performance variations 
under different user scales and diversity, and validating its general­
izability across diverse climate zones and real-time electricity pricing 
structures.
