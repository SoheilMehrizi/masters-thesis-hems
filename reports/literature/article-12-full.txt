ABSTRACT
Building Energy Management Systems (BEMS) collect and monitor building operational data for energy effi-
ciency but face challenges such as complex data management, reliability issues, and a lack of holistic approaches 
to energy efficiency. To address these limitations, this study proposes Digital Twin-enabled BEMS (DT-BEMS), 
integrating Digital Twin technology with BEMS to enhance functionality. DT-BEMS uses the Brick schema to 
create a BEMS ontology and incorporates a virtual building model defined as a mathematical representation of 
building physical behavior. This model includes three behavioral models: the energy imputation model, the 
Predicted Mean Vote (PMV) virtual model, and the occupancy model. Holistic Operational Signature (HOS) 
analyzes building operation data, identifying patterns and relationships for comprehensive efficiency evaluation. 
In DT-BEMS, a rule-based HOS classifier enables real-time operational signature identification, generating energy 
efficiency insights. The process of energy efficiency information generation was continuously updated 
throughout the building operation stages to ensure reliability and accuracy. The proposed system was validated 
using one month’s data from an office space, with data expanded at three-day intervals. DT-BEMS identified 
eight operational signature types, achieving a 97.9 % accuracy rate when using just 12 days of data compared to 
a full month. During the study, 23.5 % of operations were identified as inefficient, with energy waste during 
unoccupied periods being the most prevalent (19.9 %). The virtual model improved identification accuracy by 
4.6 % of the data. Real-time operational signature identification was implemented in the Dynamo environment, 
highlighting DT-BEMS as a practical tool for enhancing building energy management, reducing inefficiencies, 
and supporting sustainable building operations.

1. Introduction
In the face of the growing importance of addressing the climate crisis 
and achieving carbon neutrality, building energy management has 
emerged as a crucial task for a sustainable future. Approximately 40 % of 
global energy consumption occurs in buildings, accounting for a sig-
nificant portion of total carbon emissions [1]. Urbanization and eco-
nomic growth have led to a continuous increase in building energy 
consumption, making efficient energy management a necessity rather 
than a choice [2]. Therefore, improving the energy efficiency of build-
ings is essential for reducing carbon emissions and mitigating climate 
change [3]. By effectively implementing energy management strategies, 
it is possible to lower energy consumption, reduce greenhouse gas 
emissions, and ultimately achieve sustainable development objectives.
Building Energy Management Systems (BEMS) are important tools 
used to monitor and control energy consumption within buildings, 
thereby enhancing energy efficiency [4]. BEMS collect real-time data 
through sensors and metering devices, analyze energy usage patterns, 
and propose optimized energy management strategies [5]. These sys-
tems integrate various building systems such as heating, cooling, light-
ing, and ventilation to maximize energy-saving effects. As a result, they 
reduce energy consumption, lower operational costs, and improve 
occupant comfort, making BEMS an essential component of modern 
building management. Furthermore, the information on building energy 
behavior and efficiency analyzed through BEMS during the building 
operation phase can significantly contribute to policy and certification 
systems, urban management, and the advancement of zero-energy 
building design and engineering in the construction industry [6]. This 
comprehensive data supports the development of informed energy pol-
icies, helps cities manage their energy resources more efficiently, and 
drives the construction industry towards achieving higher standards of 
sustainability [7].
However, traditional BEMS have several limitations. Primarily 
relying on sensor and metering-based monitoring incurs high installa-
tion and maintenance costs. The complexity of managing large volumes 
of data from various sensors can be challenging, and any issues with data 
accuracy and reliability can compromise the effectiveness of energy 
management. Additionally, these systems are often limited to data-based 
analysis, restricting the application of advanced energy efficiency so-
lutions [8]. There is also an insufficient understanding of the interaction 
between building physical behavior and energy consumption, which 
limits the potential for maximizing energy management effectiveness. 
To address these issues, a more sophisticated and integrated approach is 
necessary. By leveraging Digital Twin technology, which has been 
actively studied in recent years, the capabilities of BEMS can be 
enhanced [9]. This approach addresses the limitations of traditional 
BEMS, enabling more efficient building management.
The concept of the Digital Twin was first introduced at University of 
Michigan presentation in 2002 aimed at establishing a Product Lifecycle 
Management (PLM) center [10]. Digital Twin, initially proposed in the 
manufacturing industry, has continuously developed and is utilized 
across various industries for purposes such as monitoring, simulation, 
optimization, and diagnosis, among others [11]. Recently, a plenty of 
papers on Digital Twin have been published in the Architecture, Engi-
neering and Construction (AEC) industry [12]. Similar to its use in 
product lifecycle management in manufacturing, Digital Twin enables 
effective building management throughout the building lifecycle in 
construction. In particular, the focus of Digital Twin is on O&M (Oper-
ation and Maintenance) stage, where Digital Twin significantly con-
tributes to the operation and management of physical buildings [13].
Digital Twin technology creates a virtual model of physical systems, 
enabling real-time monitoring and prediction based on real-time data 
[10]. Tao et al. [11] proposed a five-dimensional Digital Twin concept, 
defined by physical entities, virtual models, services, and the connec-
tions between them.These connections, or relations, encompass the ex-
change of data, information, and knowledge. By applying Digital Twin 
technology to BEMS, interaction between the physical building and the 
virtual model, based on real-time data, becomes possible. From this 
perspective, integrating Digital Twin with BEMS provides energy effi-
ciency information or knowledge that reflects the building’s physical 
behavior using real-time data. Therefore, combining Digital Twin 
technology with BEMS can help address certain limitations, such as an 
overreliance on data-based analysis and a lack of comprehensive un-
derstanding of the building’s physical behavior and energy 
consumption.
Some studies have demonstrated the potential for integrating Digital 
Twin technology with BEMS. Faham et al. [14] explored the application 
potential of Digital Twin technology in building energy management, 
highlighting its ability to monitor, optimize, and predict energy con-
sumption in real time, thus contributing to sustainable building energy 
management and cost reduction. Moreover, Grieves and Vickers [10] 
have extensively discussed the potential of Digital Twins in enhancing 
the predictability and optimization of complex systems, which is 
applicable to building energy management as well. Zakharchenko and 
Stepanets [15] proposed the concept of Digital Twin as an integrated 
platform for Building Management Systems (BMS), Building Information 
Modeling (BIM), and Internet of Things (IoT) in building automation to 
enhance energy efficiency. Hwang et al. [16] proposed a Digital 
Twin-based BEMS (DT-BEMS), discovering a potential energy savings of 
17.2 % based on data from the past month. These studies underscore the 
potential of Digital Twin technology to enhance the accuracy and 
effectiveness of energy management systems. Applying Digital Twin 
technology to building energy management allows for more accurate 
energy management through real-time interaction between physical 
buildings and virtual models. This enables the implementation of 
advanced applications and significantly improves energy efficiency. 
Digital Twins can detect system changes and anomalies in real-time, 
allowing for immediate response, and contributing to long-term en-
ergy savings and cost reductions.
Despite the potential of integrating Digital Twin technology with 
BEMS, the technical development for embedding DT into BEMS remains 
insufficient, necessitating further studies [17]. The limitations of pre-
vious studies are as follows: 
● Absence of a detailed methodology for virtual modeling: Exist-
ing research lacks robust virtual modeling methodologies and 
improvement processes for virtual models during the O&M stage. 
Virtual models must be developed in-situ and continuously to 
accommodate the variability inherent in building operations [18].
● Lack of data quality management within BEMS: There is a notable 
deficiency in strategies to address data missing due to communica-
tion errors, sensor failures, and data mapping issues within BEMS.

● Insufficiency of comprehensive energy efficiency strategies and

The purpose of this study is to propose a Digital Twin-based Building 
Energy Management System (DT-BEMS) and demonstrate the synergy 
between Digital Twin technology and BEMS by presenting the concept 
and implementation methodologies for DT-BEMS. DT-BEMS is a build-
ing management system that integrates Digital Twin with traditional 
BEMS to achieve real-time energy efficiency. The specific objectives of 
this study are outlined as follows: 
● In-situ and continuous construction of the virtual model: The in- 
situ and continuous construction of the virtual model facilitates 
ongoing adaptation and refinement, effectively reflecting real-time 
changes in the building’s operational conditions. Yoon [19] 
defined the virtual model in a Digital Twin as one that integrates 
both geometrical information and a mathematical model to describe 
the physical behavior of a building throughout its lifecycle. In-situ 
modeling involves creating more accurate models tailored to the 
specific operational conditions of the target building, compared to 
those developed using external factors such as different buildings or 
modeling environments [20]. This study utilizes operational data 
from BEMS and intrusive measurement data to construct the virtual 
model that describes the physical behavior of the building. Addi-
tionally, the performance of the virtual model continuously improves 
as data is accumulated during the building’s operational phase.
● Improvement of BEMS data quality: This research enhances 
operational data quality by representing the structure and architec-
ture of BEMS in ontology from an informatics perspective. Moreover, 
it employs machine learning-based virtual models that reflect the 
physical behavior of the building.
● Provision of comprehensive energy efficiency strategies based 
on holistic operational signature (HOS): HOS assesses the opera-
tional state comprehensively by identifying the operational signature 
of the building. This study proposes a rule-based HOS classifier to 
provide holistic energy efficiency strategies in real-time, considering 
the indoor environment, power generation by the building’s facil-
ities, and energy consumption.
● DT-BEMS from the perspective of building operations infor-
matics: This study analyzes the patterns of individual multi-source 
data and applies virtual models to generate meaningful informa-
tion. These insights are implemented as information on building 
operational states through HOS-based operational signature identi-
fication, providing a foundation for optimal building control. Addi-
tionally, DT-BEMS is updated based on the operational data 
accumulated during its operation, generating more reliable and ac-
curate information and knowledge.
DT-BEMS optimizes energy consumption, reduces maintenance 
costs, and enhances occupant comfort by processing real-time data and 
employing advanced applications. This contributes to sustainable 
building operations and supports the achievement of carbon neutrality 
goals. Additionally, this study provides a foundational reference for 
developing diverse energy management solutions utilizing Digital Twin 
technology in the future.
The remainder of this paper is composed as follows. Section 2 in-
troduces the methodology for identifying operational signatures in DT- 
BEMS, while Section 3 explains how this method was applied to real 
BEMS. Section 4 includes analytics of the results.
2. Digital Twin-based building energy management system
2.1. DT-BEMS concept
DT-BEMS is an advanced energy management system from the 
perspective of operational informatics that leverages real-time data from 
BEMS to create a virtual model that reflects the dynamic characteristics 
of a building throughout its lifecycle and provides holistic energy effi-
ciency strategies in real-time. The concept of DT-BEMS proposed in this 
study is shown in Fig. 1. DT-BEMS enables the development of energy 
efficiency strategies through (1) BEMS informatics based on ontology 
construction, (2) application of virtual models to generate behavior- 
reflective data, (3) real-time operational signatures identification by 
HOS, and (4) continuous updates on DT-BEMS across building opera-
tions. BEMS informatics is designing a metadata schema of BEMS by 
constructing BEMS ontology on Brick schema [21]. Virtual models that 
describe the energy and physical behavior of buildings are generated by 
leveraging operational data from BEMS. In the case of specific virtual 
models, the performance can be improved by implementing intrusive 
measurements, which require the temporary installation of sensors 
within the building. After constructing the virtual model, the HOS 
technique is employed as a comprehensive analytical method to provide 
energy efficiency information. HOS analyses the operation of the 
building system during the operational stage by identifying operational 
signatures [22]. This study expands the existing HOS to real-time, 
enabling real-time identification of operational signatures that 
comprehensively incorporate multi-source data. Updates on DT-BEMS 
are performed to reflect the building data more accurately. This 
approach enables the development of energy efficiency strategies 
considering the variability in building operations.
2.2. Methods
2.2.1. BEMS informatics
BEMS informatics is defined as the representation of a building’s 
architecture and the BEMS data collection system in the form of 
ontology from an informatics perspective. BEMS informatics enables a 
more precise understanding of the BEMS system and provides a foun-
dation for efficient building system management. Additionally, it ad-
dresses the mapping issues within BEMS data by representing the data 
collection system, thereby improving the quality of the collected data. 
BEMS ontology represents the control points and data collection pro-
cesses within BEMS by leveraging Brick schema. Brick schema is a 
metadata schema that standardizes semantic descriptions of the phys-
ical, logical, and virtual assets in buildings and the relationships be-
tween them [23]. Brick schema comprises three main entities: 
● Location: represents physical spaces such as floor, space, or zone
● Equipment: covers various devices like a meter, AHU, and chiller
● Point: includes specific points that generate or utilize data, including 
sensors, setpoints, and status indicators
By using these entities to represent BEMS control points and defining 
the relationships between them, BEMS ontology offers a clear and 
structured view of the BEMS. This structured representation enhances 
system understanding and supports effective building energy 
management.

2.2.2. Virtual model
Virtual model is a mathematical model that represents the physical 
behavior of building throughout its lifecycle [19]. By applying virtual 
model, it is possible to predict variables that are challenging to measure 
with physical sensors, enhance the quality of data collected within 
buildings, and generate data that reflects the physical behavior of 
buildings. In DT-BEMS environments, the virtual model includes three 
types of behavioral models: energy imputation model, occupancy 
model, and predicted mean vote (PMV) virtual model.
The energy imputation model is defined as a data imputation method 
for building energy variables, developed to predict energy consumption 
and generation throughout the building lifecycle. One of the major is-
sues related to the quality of BEMS data is the frequent occurrence of 
missing values. These missing values degrade the quality of data 
collected by BEMS, significantly impacting building energy analysis. To 
address this issue, DT-BEMS applies a machine learning-based energy 
imputation model, filling in missing values with data that reflects the 
building’s physical behavior. This approach directly enhances the 
quality of BEMS data.
The occupancy model is an occupant detection model used to infer 
the presence of occupants in specific spaces when occupant-detecting 
physical sensors are unavailable. Building occupancy is crucial for the 
efficient control of building systems and for reducing energy consump-
tion based on occupancy status [24]. While machine learning can be a 
method for occupancy modeling in environments where physical 
sensor-based occupant detection is limited, obtaining ground truth of 
occupancy data remains a significant challenge [25]. Furthermore, 
buildings with clear purposes, such as office or residential buildings, 
generally follow typical operational schedules. Consequently, this study 
leverages operational schedules and operational data analysis to 
construct the occupancy model.
The PMV virtual model is a gray-box model designed to predict the 
PMV for real occupied area. Among various indoor environmental fac-
tors, the thermal environment is the one most directly related to 
building system energy usage [26,27]. PMV is an index established by 
Fanger for the evaluation of thermal environments [28]. PMV is a widely 
used thermal environment assessment index, and its calculation is 
formula-based, making it suitable for virtual model development and 
facilitating measurement using physical sensors [29]. For these reasons, 
DT-BEMS utilizes the PMV index for indoor environment evaluation. It 
ranges from   3 to +3, where values approaching   3 indicate colder 
conditions, values nearing +3 denote warmer conditions, and values 
approximating 0 signify a state of comfort. Additionally, since indoor 
environmental sensors are usually installed on walls, it is difficult to 
accurately assess the thermal environment of the central areas where 
people reside based solely on the variables measured by these sensors. 
Therefore, developing the PMV model is essential for understanding the 
thermal environment of real occupied areas and conducting compre-
hensive energy efficiency analysis. Kim and Yoon [30] implemented a 
model to predict the PMV of the real occupied zones using intrusive data 
depending on the modeling method. Furthermore, they highlighted that 
a gray box virtual model, which corrects PMV values calculated using 
the ISO 7730 standard [31] through Bayesian estimation, showed su-
perior predictive performance. The gray-box PMV virtual model neces-
sitates intrusive measurements to gather PMV data of the target area, 
requiring temporary installation of PMV sensors.

2.2.3. Holistic operational signature
The real-time provision of energy efficiency information in DT-BEMS 
is achieved based on the HOS by utilizing the results of the virtual model 
and the operational data from BEMS. HOS is a technique for identifying 
the relationships and patterns among various variables during the 
building operation stage, based on operational signature type analysis. It 
enables a comprehensive efficiency analysis of building systems. Oper-
ational signature is an analytical approach that reveals unique correla-
tions between building energy variables and environmental variables. 
Operational signature is useful for identifying energy consumption 
patterns, operational conditions, and potential abnormal behaviors. 
Hong et al. [22]. Introduced the HOS concept, including multiple 
operational signature elements (x-OS) and representation types, for 
comprehensive operational analysis of building-side district heating 
(DH) substations. By applying HOS, it is possible to conduct 
post-evaluations of building design during the operational phase, 
analyze control quality within building systems, and assess 
energy-saving potential. In DT-BEMS, the process of real-time opera-
tional signature identification and energy efficiency information provi-
sion through HOS is illustrated in Fig. 2. This includes data 
preprocessing for pattern analysis through symbolic representation and 
modeling a rule-based HOS classifier which performs real-time identi-
fication, visualization, and interpretation of operational signatures. The 
process of data preprocessing encompasses the assignment of meaning 
to numerical data and is essential prior to HOS-based data analysis. To 
enable clustering-based HOS for real-time applications, a rule-based 
HOS classifier is required to identify operational signatures at each 
data collection interval. HOS classifier utilizes data such as building 
energy usage, solar power generation, and indoor environmental PMV to 
identify the current state according to predefined operational signature 
types. Upon completing the identification, energy efficiency insights are 
generated through visualization and analysis. For building energy usage, 
heating and cooling energy consumption are primarily analyzed as they 
have the most significant impact on indoor environmental conditions 
and represent the largest portion of energy consumption in buildings. 
The HOS classifier is subsequently applied to provide a comprehensive 
analysis of building energy efficiency, supporting informed 
decision-making for energy management and optimization.
2.2.4. Updates on DT-BEMS
DT-BEMS is continuously updated as data accumulates throughout 
the building operation phase, enabling the provision of energy efficiency 
strategies that more accurately reflect building operational data. This 
approach is designed to ensure that DT-BEMS can adapt to the vari-
ability in building operations to generate more accurate and reliable 
information and knowledge from an informatics perspective. This 
process involves continuously incorporating building operational data 
into the virtual model and HOS. The virtual model should be continu-
ously updated as operational data from BEMS accumulates to reflect the 
characteristics of physical building from an in-situ, site-centered 
modeling perspective. For example, if an occupancy model uses indoor 
CO2 concentrations to determine occupancy, the results vary as the CO2 
threshold defining occupancy is consistently updated. Modifications to 
the HOS occur during the data preprocessing stage as the criteria for 
symbolizing data are continually adjusted throughout the building’s 
operational phases. These changes subsequently refine the outcomes of 
the HOS.
3. Application of Digital Twin-based BEMS
3.1. Target space
A three-story building is utilized for the application of the DT-BEMS 
as shown in Fig. 3. DT-BEMS environment was built for second floor 
office space. A detailed description of the target building is provided in 
Table 1. The target building employs a heating and cooling system based 
on the Electric Heat Pump (EHP). The building’s energy generation 
system employs PV (photovoltaic) panels for solar power production. 
Building data utilized includes one month of BEMS data (from January 
2021, recorded at 5-min intervals) and five days of intrusive measure-
ment data. The intrusive measurement data were specifically used to 
develop the PMV virtual model, thereby deriving the PMV of the actual 
occupied area. DT-BEMS was implemented within the Dynamo envi-
ronment, enabling the real-time identification of operational signatures 
and determination of the operational status. Therefore, holistic energy 
efficiency information of building systems was created. Furthermore, 
the operational signatures identification was progressively updated to 
achieve more accuracy as data accumulated every three days.

3.2. Target BEMS informatics
BEMS ontology of the target building was represented using the Brick 
schema, with a focus on the second-floor office space. The building 
consists of three floors and a parking lot, where solar power is generated 
by PV panels. Energy consumption in the second-floor office space is 
divided into three main components: heating and cooling, lighting, and 
plug. This study primarily focused on heating and cooling usage, which 
relies on two indoor units and two outdoor units. An indoor environ-
mental sensor in the target space collects temperature, humidity, and 
CO2 data.
As shown in Fig. 4, the architecture of the BEMS system was designed 
using the Resource Description Framework (RDF) and then converted 
into a TTL file for visualization. The development of the BEMS ontology 
facilitated the integration and interpretation of data from multiple 
sources, establishing a foundation for comprehensive energy efficiency 
strategy development for building managers. Additionally, the BEMS 
ontology addresses data mapping issues by clearly defining control 
points within the BEMS.
3.3. Application of the virtual model
3.3.1. Energy data imputation model
To fill missing values in the energy dataset of the target BEMS, an 
energy data imputation model based on an MLP (Multi-Layer Percep-
tron) [32] was developed. For the HOS application, models were 
developed for building heating and cooling energy consumption data as 
well as solar power generation data. In the target BEMS system, there are 
four types of cooling and heating energy data: the usage of outdoor units 
1 and 2, and the usage of indoor units 1 and 2. However, the usage of 
indoor units is minimal and infrequently measured. As a result, energy 
data imputation models were developed for outdoor units 1 and 2, along 
with solar power generation. The model structure and input variables for 
each model are presented in Table 2.
To address missing data for the next three days, the three types of 
models were re-trained every three days as new data accumulated. For 
example, data from days 1–3 was used to impute missing values for days 
4–6. The imputed data from days 4–6 was then combined with the data 
from days 1–3 to create a dataset covering days 1–6. The model trained 
on data from days 1–6 was subsequently used to address missing data for 
days 7–9.
3.3.2. Occupancy model
The process of occupancy model consists of an initial occupancy state 
setting based on the operational schedule and occupancy updating by 
using operational data. In operational data, building energy consump-
tion and indoor environmental variables can be utilized in the occu-
pancy model. However, if energy consumption is used for occupancy 
estimation, it becomes difficult to detect energy waste in unoccupied 
situations. Additionally, among the environmental variables measured 
by indoor sensors, CO2 concentration is most strongly correlated with 
occupancy status [33,34]. Therefore, as illustrated in Fig. 5, this study 
constructed an occupancy model based on the operational schedule and 
CO2 concentration.
Since the target space is an office environment, daytime was set as 
the initial occupied state, and nighttime was set as the unoccupied state. 
In the initial occupied state, if the absolute CO2 concentration is low 
(below the average value during occupied periods) and shows a 
decreasing trend, it was updated to unoccupied state. In contrast, if the 
CO2 concentration during initial unoccupied state is higher than the 
average CO2 concentration during unoccupied time and increasing, the 
status was changed to occupied. As the average CO2 concentration 
changes with data accumulation, the occupancy model result was 
updated every three days.

3.4. HOS application
The application of HOS involves binning data to symbolize each 
segment, followed by identifying the operational signature type using 
the HOS classifier.
3.4.1. Data binning
Data binning in DT-BEMS refers to the process of segmenting various 
data of BEMS to assign meaning to each segment. Data binning is 
valuable for simplifying data, allowing for a holistic consideration of 
diverse datasets. In this study, data binning was performed on three 
types of data. Absolute criteria were applied to PMV data obtained 
through the application of the PMV virtual model, while relative criteria 
were used for energy consumption data and solar power generation 
data.
(1) Absolute criteria
Data binning using absolute criteria is a method that performs seg-
mentation based on fixed boundaries according to predefined standards. 
PMV data was binned using absolute criteria based on its definition. The 
PMV range of   0.5 to 0.5 was defined as the comfort zone, and the data 
was divided into three segments based on this range. If the PMV value 
was below   0.5, it was labeled as cold, and if it was above 0.5, it was 
labeled as hot. Since the data used in this study was collected during the 
winter, PMV values below the comfort zone (cold) were interpreted as 
indicating an uncomfortable state, while PMV values above the comfort 
zone (hot) were considered as an over-comfort state.
(2) Relative criteria
Data binning using relative criteria refers to the method of estab-
lishing data intervals based on the distribution of existing data values. 
Due to the significant variability of BEMS energy data, it is challenging 
to establish fixed boundaries. Therefore, using relative criteria is ad-
vantageous. For the binning of energy consumption and solar power 
generation data, GMM (Gaussian Mixture Model) and K-means clus-
tering were utilized as shown in Fig. 7. GMM was used to identify the 
boundary between non-operating and operating states. For the oper-
ating state, K-means clustering was then applied to determine the 
boundaries for categorizing the data into three levels: low, middle, and 
high. Based on these boundaries, energy consumption data and solar 
power generation data were classified into four clusters. To continuously 
update the boundaries and better reflect the characteristics of the data,

3.4.2. Operational signatures identification using HOS classifier
After applying data binning, operational signatures for each time 
were identified in real-time using the rule-based HOS classifier shown in 
Fig. 8. The types of operational signatures are defined as shown in 
Table 6. Initially, the operational status of the building system is 
determined using threshold values of energy consumption obtained 
from data binning. When the building system is in operation, the oc-
cupancy model is applied to ascertain whether the space is occupied. If 
the target space is unoccupied, it is classified as Energy waste. Given 
that the target area was heated, employing techniques such as radiant 
heating may imply that energy consumption during periods of unoccu-
pancy is not inherently inefficient. Nevertheless, as the current target 
area is being heated by the EHP, considerable energy usage during un-
occupied periods was deemed wasteful consumption. If the target space 
was occupied, the operating status is then categorized into one of five 
categories based on PMV, energy consumption, and solar generation 
data. For example, a situation where the PMV exceeds 0.5 with high 
energy usage indicates excessive heating. This condition is classified as 
Recommendation for reduced operation. Conversely, if the PMV is 
below   0.5 (indicating cold and uncomfortable) and energy usage is 
high, it suggests that the heating load is excessive, resulting in classifi-
cation as Inefficient operation.
Since the results of behavioral models and data binning were upda-
ted every three days, the identification outcomes also followed a three- 
day update cycle. Datasets accumulated in three-day intervals were used 
to identify the full month’s data, and the results of each dataset were 
compared. Additionally, a comparison was made between the results 
obtained before and after applying PMV virtual model and occupancy 
model.

3.6. DT-BEMS in Dynamo interface
The DT-BEMS environment was developed using the Dynamo inter-
face with a focus on real-time application. Fig. 10 represents the 
workflow of DT-BEMS for real-time operational signature identification. 
Energy data and indoor environment data were integrated, and three 
behavioral models within the virtual building model were applied. After 
reflecting physical building behavior through the virtual building 
model, data binning was conducted to symbolize the operational data.

and energy efficiency information was generated. Operational signature 
was determined for each timestamp in real-time, and after a one-day 
analysis, the data was visualized using tools such as heatmaps and pie

4.4. Limitations and future directions
This study has several limitations. The occupancy model applied to 
the target space determined occupancy status based on the operational 
schedule and CO2 concentration in a constrained sensing environment. 
However, further refinement of the model is necessary. Additionally, the 
HOS classifier was designed with a focus on the characteristics of the 
target building, which employs an EHP-based heating and cooling sys-
tem. Moreover, it was only applied for a month (January) during winter. 
Finally, while DT-BEMS effectively identified operational signatures and 
generated energy efficiency information, the study lacks a quantitative 
assessment of its impact on actual energy savings and operational cost 
reductions.
Considering these limitations, future research should proceed as 
follows. First, ground truth data of occupancy status should be estab-
lished by integrating passive infrared (PIR) sensors or installing cameras 
in the target space, enabling the development of a more advanced oc-
cupancy model. Second, the HOS classifier that can be generally applied 
across various building systems should be developed and validated 
under diverse climate conditions to ensure adaptability. Third, DT-BEMS 
should be directly integrated with the building control system, incor-
porating an ontology-based AI agent system. By leveraging BEMS 
informatics, the AI agent can be specialized for the target building sys-
tem and domain, thereby enabling building control based on operational 
signature types. This integration will facilitate the quantitative evalua-
tion of energy savings and operational cost reductions. Additionally, 
integrating with control algorithms such as model predictive control 
(MPC) can further maximize energy-efficient operation.
5. Conclusion
In this study, the concept of DT-BEMS was proposed and imple-
mented in the Dynamo environment to verify the operational signatures 
of the target space in real-time, thereby generating building energy ef-
ficiency information. In BEMS informatics, the representation of the 
BEMS data collection system and building structure in an ontology 
format was completed to promote the digitalization of both the building 
and the BEMS system. From the Digital Twin perspective, three types of 
virtual models were developed. The energy imputation model improved 
data quality by filling in missing values with data that reflect the 
building’s physical behavior. The PMV virtual model enabled the esti-
mation of PMV values within actual occupied zones. Additionally, an 
occupancy model was implemented to determine occupancy status 
based on operational schedules and CO2 data. By integrating the out-
comes of the virtual models with data binning for pattern analysis, the 
HOS classifier diagnosed the operational signatures of specific time 
periods. Through this approach, DT-BEMS generated information and 
knowledge about building energy efficiency, with updates occurring 
every three days to improve accuracy and reliability. In the target 
building, using 12 days of data achieved 97.9 % similarity in identifying 
operational signatures compared to the results obtained using a full 
month’s dataset. When analyzing the operational signatures over a full 
month, 23.5 % of operations were identified as inappropriate. Further-
more, by comparing operational signature diagnostics before and after 
applying the virtual models, the importance of the virtual model was 
emphasized.
In conclusion, this study successfully established an information 
system for analyzing building operational states by leveraging real-time 
multi-source data from BEMS through DT-BEMS. Generally, diagnosing 
a building’s operational state and identifying appropriate response 
strategies require continuous expert-level decision-making in the 
building environment domain. The energy efficiency strategy proposed 
in this study is initially designed by experts and evolves autonomously 
during the operational phase. This approach has the potential to 
significantly reduce the cost and time required for building energy ef-
ficiency improvements. Furthermore, this system establishes a founda-
tion for effective building control that considers indoor environmental 
conditions.
